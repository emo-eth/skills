# Poster specification

Use this as the generation/editing brief after substituting known metadata.

## Composition

Create one independent 4:3 landscape **rubber-stamp travel field-note poster** from the attached source photo. Do not make a multi-image collage.

- **Left, about 58%:** preserve the original photograph faithfully. Keep subject identity, terrain, architecture, plants, people, spatial relationships, natural light, realistic texture, and original color atmosphere. Natural cropping is allowed for layout. Do not stretch, distort, move, replace, or redraw subjects. Apply only restrained editorial color grading and extremely fine film grain.
- **Right, about 42%:** warm off-white aged paper with subtle fibers, natural grain, slight handling marks, and a matte surface. Keep extensive unprinted negative space. Do not draw a visible central divider.

## Stamp abstraction

Analyze the source and reduce its most location-specific relationships into one small multicolor carved-rubber stamp: key silhouette, building structure, terrain direction, plant gesture, road, shoreline, or water relationship. Keep only the minimum information needed to recognize the subject and place. Remove crowds, vehicles, repetitive windows/buildings, tiny vegetation, ornament, and unrelated background.

Place the stamp in the lower-middle of the paper area. Its height should occupy about 30–38% of the right panel. It must remain a small field-note impression, not a full landscape illustration, ordinary logo, or enlarged badge.

Choose the organization from the source:

- Landmark architecture: distinctive outer silhouette plus only the main roof, dome, arch, tower, or structure.
- Hillside settlement: a few stepped color blocks following the terrain.
- Coast: mountain direction, settlement layers, shoreline, and a few broken water lines.
- City distance: main skyline, one landmark, and at most one or two distant ridges.
- Nature: main mountain, tree, shore, water, or road directions.
- Important foreground obstruction: retain its silhouette only when it defines the source composition.

## Ink and material

Extract 2–4 muted spot colors from the source. Favor photo-derived charcoal, deep green, brick red, ochre, gray-blue, or gray-brown rather than a fixed palette. Allow one small accent color.

Every color layer must look separately hand-stamped: carved texture, uneven hatch width, broken contours, dry missing ink, paper showing through, granular ink, pressure variation, local ghosting, and about 1–2 mm natural color misregistration. Edges must not be digitally smooth. The result should resemble actual carved rubber pressed onto old paper, not a filtered photo, smooth vector illustration, line-art logo, or screen-print simulation.

## Text

Use only:

- exact English location name: "{LOCATION}"
- "No. {NUMBER}"
- three short English keywords: "{KEYWORD_1} / {KEYWORD_2} / {KEYWORD_3}"
- Gregorian year: "{YEAR}"

Place the copy below the stamp or in adjacent blank space in a small restrained typewriter face with slight mechanical imperfection. Spell every word exactly. Do not add slogans, brands, captions, or decoration.

## Tone

Quiet, restrained, tactile, and place-specific—like an architect's, travel writer's, or naturalist's collected field note. The photograph records the scene; the stamp preserves only its most memorable recognizable relationships.

## Exclude

Visible center divider, circular seal, Chinese red seal, postage perforations, wax seal, sticker collage, souvenir template, smooth vector logo, generic city icon, exhaustive building copy, dense engraving, childish craft styling, cartoon, 3D render, plastic texture, smooth digital gradient, excessive saturation, excessive text, decorative clutter, or any redrawing/change to the original photograph.

## Validation prompt for revisions

Compare the result to the source photo and the acceptance checks in `SKILL.md`. Name the single largest failure. Revise only that failure while preserving everything else.
